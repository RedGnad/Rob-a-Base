## 1. Player Promise

**One-line promise.**

You have a base on a public street. You display loot to earn while you sleep, and anyone can walk in and rob it.

**One familiar comparison.**

It is an idle tycoon where the shelf is in public: your income is on display, and the other players are the risk, or the alliance.

**Why this game.**

The tycoon-and-steal loop is fun and addictive, it gives players a strong reason to return, and it creates social standing between them. We also wanted to prove that a full game, and not a demo, fits one thumb on the Decentraland mobile app.

## 2. First Minutes and How to Play

| Time | Player experience |
|---|---|
| **0 to 5 seconds after control** | You spawn on the plaza facing a street of towers, each with its owner's name lit on the sign. The ground ahead is green and your pad shows one button: PLACE. |
| **5 to 10 seconds** | You tap PLACE. Your base lands where you stand, a crate already waiting inside. |
| **10 to 60 seconds** | Three taps break the crate open, an item rolls out with its rarity, you tap SHELVE, and its coin counter starts to climb. |
| **1 to 3 minutes** | You tap COLLECT and the coins are yours: open, shelve, collect is the loop. The banner names your next goal, a second storey. You explore the street and walk into other players' bases. |
| **3 to 10 minutes** | A belt of crates runs past the plaza: you buy one, it walks itself home, and anyone watching can outbid it. A neighbour's shelf offers ROB: hold six seconds, the owner is alerted by name, and the way out is a shootout. A boss spawns, a rush repaints the sky, and the shop and prestige open. |
| **Natural stopping point** | Your shelves keep earning while you are away, and the exit banner shows the offline clock filling and today's seven-day reward step. |

**Player-facing How to Play.**

- Open crates, shelve loot, collect coins
- Coins buy storeys, crates and gear
- Anything on show can be robbed

## 3. Core Loop

| # | Step (verb) | What the player does | Why do it again? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Open | Tap the action button three times on a crate, the lid bursts and the item is revealed with its rarity and mutation, the item goes to your hands | The roll is different every time: 7 rarities and 14 mutations, and the rare rolls are what the street sees. |
| 2 | Shelve | Tap SHELVE at a free slot, the item takes its place on the storey and a coin counter appears above it, your income per second rises | Income is cumulative and permanent, and every shelved item is also bait. |
| 3 | Collect | Tap COLLECT on the banner, the coins fly to your total, the counters reset | Coins are the only way to buy the next storey, the next crate and the gear. |
| 4 | Rob | Tap a neighbour's item and stay within twelve metres for six seconds plus two per rarity. The owner is alerted by name the moment you touch it, and if they are away the alert waits at their door. You carry the item home under fire from their sentries | Their best shelf is always worth more than your next crate, it can be taken whether they are online or asleep, and they now owe you a visit. |
| 5 | Defend | Tap LOCK on your door for sixty seconds, arm a sentry on a storey, lay a trap, cloak, taser or bomb | Everything you built is on display, and the alert tells you when it is happening. |

| | |
|---|---|
| **One complete loop takes** | About forty seconds, from a full crate to coins collected. The earlier payoff is the reveal at ten seconds: the rarity roll lands before any of the money does. |
| **Decision, challenge or expression** | Shelve the item or sell it, which storey to fill, spend on a storey or on gear, lock the door now or keep the cooldown, farm your own crates or go and take a better item from a neighbour. During a theft: hold the six seconds in the open, or wait for the owner's sentry to run out of charges. Players who tested it singled out the escape shootout as the most fun part of the game. |
| **Shortest satisfying visit / typical session** | 3 / 15 minutes. Three minutes is collect, one crate, one shelf. |
| **Why repetition 10 differs from repetition 1** | The street is the content: other players' towers, shelves and prices change between visits. On top of that, the roll table (7 rarities by 14 mutations), rush events on ten themes every fifteen minutes, luck windows that double the rolls, and thirty prestige tiers. Players who tested it also named the items themselves as the reason they kept opening crates, which is what the table is there for. |

**Pillars.**

1. Nothing on show is safe. Income requires exposure.
2. One thumb plays the whole game, one action button whose glyph is the verb.
3. Your neighbours are the level. Their towers are the content and their names are the memory.

## 4. Why Players Come Back

### 4.1 The next-day (D1) sentence

> "A player who enjoyed their first session returns the next day (D1) because Hook 1, the shelf income earned while they are away, runs at 35 percent and stops at 24 hours: the exit banner shows that clock, and the welcome-back screen pays it out the moment they walk in."

### 4.2 The progression chain

> **Repeatable action, persistent reward, new capability or status or social role, new decision or challenge**

| Moment | What persists or has been built? | What becomes possible next? | How can another player tell? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **End of first session** | A base standing on the street with the owner's name on the sign, two or three items shelved and earning, the first daily quest closed | Buy the second storey, six more slots, and set the first lock | The tower and its sign are public, the shelves are visible from the street, and the top eight are on the world leaderboard |
| **End of first week** | Several storeys up to twelve, a sentry armed per storey, gear owned out of eight types, one full seven-day reward cycle, collection progress on 98 item combinations | You can defend, raid another base and survive a shootout, and you are now worth robbing | Tower height reads from anywhere on the plaza, sentries fire in public, the leaderboard names you |
| **Week 3 and beyond, what takes more than two weeks** | Prestige, thirty tiers: the base resets and the multiplier is permanent. The full collection cannot be rushed, because the rolls and the luck windows gate it | Higher prestige unlocks the upper storeys and a longer free lock, ten seconds more per tier | Prestige shows on the sign, and a twelve-storey tower is a landmark on the street |

You come back the next evening and the welcome-back screen pays out the night: your shelves ran while you slept. Two items are missing and the journal names who took them, so the revenge mark is already on them. You spend the night's money on the fourth storey, arm a sentry on it, and leave the door unlocked because the lock is on cooldown and the good item is upstairs.

Currency: coins are earned by shelf income and by selling items, and spent on crates, storeys, gear, sentries and prestige. They leave the game on prestige and when an item is stolen, since the shelf that produced it stops. Main abuse risk: a player using a second account to feed themselves easy thefts. The state is server-authoritative and every theft alerts the owner by name, so the pattern is visible, but nothing blocks it today.

### 4.3 Two return hooks

| Selected hook | Exact trigger or timing | What the player anticipates | Reminder channel plus no-reminder fallback |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1.** Appointment timer, the offline shelf income | Shelves earn at 35 percent of normal income while the player is away, and the clock stops at 24 hours. Whatever happened meanwhile waits too: every theft and every sentry report is written to the absent owner's profile and read on arrival, and a base robbed while its owner was away seals for up to eight hours, so a sleeping player loses one item and not the shelf | The payout waiting at the door, the name of whoever came, and what their sentries did about it | Fallback without any reminder: the exit banner shows the offline clock and the cap before the player leaves, and the welcome-back screen pays it out on arrival |
| **2.** Recurring scheduled event, the grand rush | Every day at 20:00 UTC, ten minutes, double tempo on every crate and every shelf | The one hour where the street is crowded and everything pays twice | Event listing on decentraland.org, plus a Community post. Fallback without reminder: a countdown to the grand rush on the plaza sign, and random rushes of five minutes every fifteen minutes in between |

## 5. Social by Design

| | |
|---|---|
| **The repeatable social loop** | Player A touches an item on Player B's shelf, which alerts B by name at once. B answers: run back, shoot the thief, seal the door or arm a sentry. Either the item lands in A's tower or B recovers it inside the twenty-second window. The outcome is public on both towers and in the journal, and B keeps a revenge mark on A for up to eight hours, which is the reason to come back to them. |
| **The disappearance test** | Less than we assumed, and playtests corrected us here. If every other player vanished but their towers and data stayed, robbing still works: the towers stand, their shelves are stocked, their sentries still fire and take their tithe, and the alert is written to the absent owner's profile for whenever they return. What dies is the live half: the chase, the shootout, retaliation in person, the crowd bonus and outbidding a crate in transit. Feedback from a Decentraland community member who played it was that robbing bases whose owners were not there was their favourite part, which is why the asynchronous layer is designed rather than tolerated. |
| **From strangers to a group** | Every base carries its owner's name on a lit sign, so the street reads as people, not props. Walk within twelve metres of a shelf and the pad itself offers ROB, and standing near other players raises the income banner. No text is needed anywhere: the pad is glyphs only. |
| **Recognition and continuity** | The name is above the avatar, on the base sign, and in the theft alert. What persists between two players: the revenge mark (up to eight hours), the journal of the last forty events, the top eight of the world leaderboard, and a tower they recognise. A player who robbed you yesterday is a name you check for when you arrive. |
| **Quiet hours and player counts** | When nobody else is online, a solo player can still do everything, stealing included: the other towers stand, and their shelves and sentries are live. Asynchronous social play therefore works at 1 online player plus one standing base, and the live half (chase, shootout, retaliation in person, crowd bonus) becomes viable at 2 players. The ideal group is 5, because each player present adds 15 percent income to everyone, capped at 60 percent with four others. `TBD:` peak concurrent players. The game has been played in public since 27 August, through a buildathon and its judging, but we publish no count we cannot source: the program dashboard is the source, and it will read this world once the studio is registered. The server holds a ledger of 385 objects, which stands about 25 bases at once, and evicts the base of the longest-absent owner, never a player who is present. A solo arrival meets someone by design because there is one street and one plaza: the belt, the shop and the rushes all happen in the same place. |
| **Drop-in / drop-out** | A newcomer is playing in five seconds: place the base on any green square, and if the square was just taken the server puts it on the nearest free one and says so, never a refusal. When a player leaves, their tower stays up, keeps earning and stays robbable. Nothing breaks if anyone leaves mid-theft: the item is either carried home or dropped and recoverable. |
| **Visible play (the bystander test)** | You see a player run down a ramp with someone else's item over their head while a sentry fires at their back. |
| **Shareable play (the memorable moment)** | The door seals one second before you reach the shelf, with the owner's name on the sign and the timer running in your face. |
| **Bring-a-friend** | You want a specific person when your shelves are full and the street is empty: their presence alone pays you 15 percent more. Once they are there, the game has its other half, because a theft only means something when the owner is someone who will come back for you. |

## 6. Mobile-First

**Every core-loop verb on touch.**

| Core-loop verb | How it works with touch controls |
|---|---|
| Open | Tap the single action button three times, its glyph is a fist |
| Shelve | Tap the same button at a free slot, its glyph becomes a shelf |
| Collect | Tap COLLECT on the bottom banner |
| Rob | Tap the item, then stay inside twelve metres while the bar fills; one glyph, one timer |
| Defend | Tap LOCK on the door post, tap ARM on a sentry, and the four gear discs are one tap each |

**UI plan.**

One context action button in the right thumb zone, whose glyph always shows the verb it will perform, plus four gear discs and a single bottom banner for money, income and goals. Every glyph is generated by a script and brought to one optical extent, so no verb reads heavier than another, and the jump and glider glyphs are the mobile client's own files, so our pad and the client's pad speak one language. Desktop gets the same pad, scaled up.

**Performance.**

Target: 60 fps on recommended desktop hardware, 30 fps on the Decentraland mobile app. The scene holds itself inside the mobile content limits by construction, not by hope: the server keeps a ledger of 385 objects and charges each base what it really costs, and the level of detail is driven by the same budget, so the nearest bases render in full and far ones as silhouettes. A storey is 4 merged meshes instead of 23 objects, and item models are shared. `TBD:` a frame-rate figure on a named phone at a named player count. The scene has run on the mobile app in public since 27 August and its content budget is enforced at runtime, but the measurements logged so far are desktop plus handset checks by a tester.

**Desktop-only dependencies.**

None. The game was designed on the mobile client first and the desktop client runs the same code and the same pad, scaled up.

## 7. World, Look and Story

**Story / world.**

A public street where every tenant pays for their tower by putting their loot where everyone can see it. Nothing is insured, and the only law is the door lock and whoever is standing on your ramp.

**Visual direction.**

Towers are flat coloured slabs with a lit owner sign, so the street reads as a row of names at a distance. Objectives are the only lit things: shelved items glow on their slot, a sealed door goes red, an armed sentry glows, and the item in a thief's hands is held above their head so a bystander can follow the theft across the plaza on a small screen. Our signature shot: a twelve-storey tower at night, its shelves lit, one avatar carrying a stolen item down the ramp.

## 8. Audience and Comparables

**Primary player plus arrival context.**

For players who already enjoy Roblox-style tycoon and steal games or idle progression, arriving alone from Discover or the mobile app, looking for a three-minute visit that pays out on a street where someone might rob them.

**How the first group arrives.**

The first real groups come from Discover and the World's Places listing plus the audience of the Friendzone buildathon where the game placed 4th, and then from an Event listing on the daily 20:00 UTC grand rush, which concentrates players at a fixed time. Overlap is cheap here because the social threshold is two players, not a full lobby.

**Deliberately not for.**

Players who want their progress protected; here everything on display can be taken by someone else.

### Comparables

| | Comparable A, outside Decentraland: Steal a Brainrot (Roblox) | Comparable B, outside Decentraland: Clash of Clans |
|---|---|---|
| What we observed works | Public shelves as the whole economy, a theft alert that names the thief, a one-tap loop a child understands in ten seconds, and a collection absurd enough to be worth showing | Offline production collected on return, raiding other players' bases, a shield that protects an absent player, and revenge as a named relationship rather than a score |
| What does not fit our audience or context, and why | It lives in small private instances, so the thief is a stranger you never meet again, and its social layer runs on text chat, which we cannot assume in Decentraland | Its raid runs against a snapshot of a base, so the owner can never be there and the defence is a layout rather than a play. Its progression is also paced by paid timers |
| What we will do differently | One shared persistent street with owner names, server-authoritative theft, and an offline income cap that gives a reason to return the next day | One street, both modes and never a snapshot: the theft is live and in person when the owner is home, asynchronous against their standing tower when they are not. The shield is not bought, it is granted by a theft and scaled by how long the owner had really been away, one minute if they are standing there and eight hours if they are asleep, and revenge walks through it |

## 9. 4 Week Plan (v0 scope)

The loop, the authoritative server, the twelve storeys, the gear, the rushes, the boss, the raids and the asynchronous theft are live and have been played in public since 27 August, through a buildathon and its judging where the game placed 4th. The four weeks are not spent proving the loop. They go into the three things the shipped version is thinnest on: the environment's visuals, the onboarding, and the depth of the gameplay that already exists.

| Week | What is playable / done |
|---|---|
| 1 Environment visuals | The street becomes a place instead of a field: ground and plaza materials, night lighting on the lit signs and the shelves, a horizon that closes the world edge, and districts so an address is memorable. Every addition re-measured against the content budget, since decor already spends 106 of the 385 objects and the mobile material cap is 400 |
| 2 Onboarding | Time real arrivals against the 5/10 rule, first useful action under five seconds and the goal stated under ten, and cut what fails. Two known mobile faults go first: the gear roulette sits too far right and the timer on the door post disagrees with the banner by a second. A returning player sees the waiting alerts and the offline payout before anything else |
| 3 Gameplay depth | Tune the exposure balance on measurement, the share of standing bases a lone arrival can actually rob per hour, deepen the theft counterplay that already exists (the sentry charge is spent on the robbed storey, so finding the soft floor is the attacker's read), and verify the ledger's eviction at about 25 bases standing |
| 4 The pass together | Mobile playtest with a frame-rate figure on a named phone, the look locked, the first week of daily grand rushes scheduled and announced, and D1 and D7 read on the program dashboard as the scoreboard rather than on our own counters |

**What keeps the experience changing after launch.**

- Without building a new level, we can rotate the three daily quests out of nine every day, and the rush themes out of ten every five minutes.
- If an update is skipped, the roll table (7 rarities by 14 mutations), the luck windows and the other players' towers still create variation.
- Progression resets only by choice: prestige clears the base and keeps the multiplier and the collection. A new or returning player can compete within three minutes, because the starter crate and the daily reward are immediate and theft ignores tower height: a one-storey player can take the best item on the street.
- One player behaviour that would change what we build next: if players lock their doors and stop shelving their best items, the income has to move further onto display risk.

**Not building in v0.**

1. Tradable or on-chain rewards. Standing non-goal: never tradable loot, because it turns a theft into real-value theft and the game stops being a game.
2. A second street or map. First cut to come back in v1 if v0 hits its numbers.
3. Voice or free text chat. The pad stays glyphs only, so the game does not depend on a shared language.

**Top risk plus fallback.**

Exposure balance, not concurrency. Because theft also works against absent owners, the game lives or dies on how much of the street is robbable at a given hour: seal too much and an arriving player finds nothing to take, seal too little and sleeping players get farmed and stop coming back. Today that balance is one function, ramping the post-theft shield from one minute for an owner standing there to eight hours for one away four hours or more, with a revenge mark that walks through any shield. Fallback: it is a single tunable curve, so v0 measures the share of robbable bases per hour and retunes it without touching the loop; if the street still reads as sealed, a base becomes robbable once per visitor instead of once per window. The grand rush at a fixed hour and the Event listing remain the answer for the live half.

**One last question.**

Section 5, the disappearance test. We first wrote that the theft half of the game needed a second player online, and playtesting said otherwise: robbing an absent neighbour's tower is what people liked most, so the asynchronous layer became a design decision instead of a consolation.
